## Disagreements and tensions that matter managerially

The largest unresolved tension is the **warm-up retrieval timestamp and counting contract**. The Codex/local record proposes a concrete `raw_retrieval_start = 2022-12-01T00:00:00Z`, counts accepted observed regular-session bars, and requires at least 50 valid pre-start bars per symbol. The deep-research record agrees on rejecting raw API row-count logic, but it recommends an explicit UTC timestamp-window method anchored to the calendar rather than endorsing a specific retrieval timestamp. The audit is stricter still: it separates retrieval-start counting from warm-up sufficiency, prohibits adaptive per-symbol starts, and says the **exact UTC timestamp remains a blocker** until choices around calendar, timezone/order, and bar semantics are finalized. In other words, the three views agree on the method family, but not yet on a final literal timestamp. citeturn17view1turn18view6turn18view7turn18view2turn19view2

Another material tension is the **timezone-order and bar-edge contract**. The local recommendation says to parse aware UTC, map to New York/XNYS, then filter, then serialize UTC. The deep-research record recommends a clearer **UTC-first** contract: normalize to timezone-aware UTC immediately, build the XNYS schedule in UTC, filter in UTC, and only derive New York session labels secondarily. The audit’s recommended order is closer to the deep-research approach than to the local one, because it explicitly treats provider timestamps as UTC, uses XNYS session opens and closes in UTC, and warns against manual clock-string filtering. This is not a philosophical disagreement; it affects DST boundaries, early closes, and whether session eligibility is defined from instants or from derived local clock labels. citeturn17view1turn18view6turn18view2turn19view0turn20view2

The most important semantic issue identified by the audit is the difference between **provider-defined Alpaca one-hour bars** and an explicitly **09:30-aligned NYSE hourly regime**. All three views reject silent 09:30 realignment as the default path. But the exact consequence at session edges is still not fully harmonized. The audit recommends retaining only provider intervals **fully contained** within the approved XNYS session and excluding partial session-edge bars. The local recommendation says the market-open partial provider hour requires a precise reviewed overlap rule. The deep-research record strongly supports left-edge provider-defined hourly semantics, but it is less operationally specific than the audit on full-containment versus overlap handling. This issue is central because it can change first-row timestamps, last-row timestamps, and row counts without anyone changing the ticker list or nominal timeframe. citeturn17view1turn18view3turn18view6turn19view0turn19view1

The last major tension is the **physical Parquet and writer profile**. All three views agree that the contract must be fully explicit. They do **not** fully agree on every literal. The local recommendation specifies `Parquet 2.6`, `use_dictionary = FALSE`, and `row_group_size = 65536`. The external deep-research record recommends a more interoperability-oriented profile centered on `version='1.0'`, `coerce_timestamps='us'`, and explicit nullable integer/string policy. The audit sits between them: it says pandas `3.0.2` and pyarrow `24.0.0` are defensible candidates, recommends freezing PyArrow 24 defaults such as `parquet_format_version = 2.6`, `compression = snappy`, `data_page_version = 1.0`, `use_dictionary = TRUE`, and `write_statistics = TRUE`, but also says exact `row_group_size` and timestamp-coercion settings still require literal managerial selection. This is the most obvious place where the executive record can choose the path class now but still leave some literals to the later contract-resolution step. citeturn18view4turn18view7turn19view1turn19view2turn20view3

### Risks and limitations

The principal limitation is straightforward: **this is not a byte-identical recovery case**. The index says byte-identical recovery is not proven. The audit says the original dataset has not been recovered and must not be described as historically identical. The local recommendation says the future output needs a new dataset identity and must not be represented as recovered v3.07 bytes. The deep-research record says the strongest externally supportable framing is a superseding governed Alpaca-aligned reconstruction contract, not exact original recovery. citeturn17view0turn17view1turn18view0turn18view7turn19view1

A second limitation is that **public validation evidence is only secondary evidence**. The deep-research record says the public validation repository can help anchor an Alpaca/IEX/1H managerial decision, but it does not publicly document the historical adjustment policy, market-calendar implementation, or full Parquet writer contract, so it cannot govern the local reconstruction contract by itself. The index makes the same distinction by classifying that record as supporting external research evidence rather than the governing local decision. citeturn17view0turn18view0turn18view1

A third limitation is semantic drift risk. IEX versus SIP can materially change bars; omitted defaults can drift with account entitlements or SDK behavior; session-edge handling can alter row counts; and physical Parquet settings can alter file bytes without changing the apparent business meaning of the dataset. The audit is especially strong on this point, warning that source defaults, calendar versioning, expected-slot circularity, and writer settings all create nontrivial reproducibility risk if left implicit. citeturn17view1turn18view0turn19view2
