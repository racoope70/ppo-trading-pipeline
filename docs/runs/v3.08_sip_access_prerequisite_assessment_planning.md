## 5. Assessment objectives

A future assessment would determine:

- whether SIP entitlement is required and available;

- whether separate licensing requirements apply;

- whether any cost or purchase is required;

- whether access prerequisites can be proven sufficiently before bounded technical execution is considered.

## 10. Separation of assessment stages

### Stage A — Public-document research

May occur only after separate authorization and may inspect official public materials only. It may not inspect an account, purchase anything, create a client, call an API, or access market data.

### Stage B — Account-specific inspection

May occur only if separately authorized after public evidence is reviewed. It must be read-only and may inspect entitlement, permission, plan, subscription, or billing status only within authorized scope. It may not change the account or purchase anything.

### Stage C — Account or subscription change and purchase consideration

Requires a separate decision evaluating cost, recurrence, cancellation, downgrade, licensing, and expected research value. No automatic purchase or upgrade is permitted.

### Stage D — Bounded SIP technical-execution authorization

May be considered only after prerequisites are proven satisfied and must separately authorize client creation, API calls, SIP data access, and provider-coverage testing.

### Stage E — Bounded SIP provider-coverage execution

Must target only the governed technical question and remain separate from candidate acceptance, contract replacement, and dataset generation.

## 11. Fail-closed criteria

The later assessment must fail closed when:

- entitlement remains unknown;
- required permissions remain unknown;
- plan or subscription requirements remain unknown;
- licensing or permitted-use restrictions remain unknown;
- conflicting authoritative evidence is unresolved;
- public evidence is insufficient and account inspection is not authorized;
- account-specific evidence is required but unavailable;
- a purchase is required but not separately authorized;
- intended storage, research, model-development, paper-trading, or production use cannot be shown as permitted;
- historical access requirements remain unresolved;
- evidence does not clearly apply to the relevant account or intended use;
- prerequisites cannot be proven without client creation or a data request before technical execution is authorized; or
- source provenance cannot be recorded adequately.

Fail-closed means:

```text
READY_FOR_BOUNDED_SIP_TECHNICAL_EXECUTION_AUTHORIZATION_CONSIDERATION = NO
```

## 12. Stop conditions

Stop immediately if:

- the assessment would exceed its authorization;
- a public source must be consulted before public research is authorized;
- an account must be opened or inspected before account inspection is authorized;
- an account or subscription change, payment, or purchase is requested;
- credentials would need to be entered into code;
- client creation, an API call, market-data access, or provider-coverage testing would occur;
- contract replacement or relaxation would be required;
- raw, candidate, dataset, validation, training, order, deployment, or tagging work would begin;
- evidence conflicts materially; or
- sensitive information cannot be safely excluded.

## 13. Decision rules for later assessment

- One weak or indirect source cannot override conflicting direct authoritative evidence.
- General public documentation cannot by itself prove account-specific entitlement.
- An account display cannot by itself establish broad licensing or permitted-use rights unless it expressly addresses them.
- Unknown and conflicting results remain unresolved.
- Absence of evidence is not evidence that access is unavailable.
- Failure to establish access is not equivalent to a zero-row provider response.
- Technical coverage cannot be inferred without an authorized provider request.
- A paid option being available would not itself authorize purchase.
- Satisfying access prerequisites would not authorize contract replacement, candidate acceptance, or dataset generation.
