## 5. Authorized account facts

Only the following facts may be inspected after the authorization becomes effective:

1. Current Alpaca market-data plan name or tier.
2. Existing account type, when displayed.
3. Existing professional, nonprofessional, or market-data classification.
4. Existing geographic or eligibility information relevant to market-data access.
5. Existing entitlement or permission for delayed historical SIP data.
6. Existing historical market-data API rate-limit entitlement, when displayed without making an API call.
7. Existing subscription status.
8. Existing account-visible recurring plan price or direct plan charge.
9. Existing billing-cycle label or renewal status, excluding payment-instrument details.
10. Titles, identifiers, effective dates, and directly relevant provisions of agreements already governing the account, only when viewable without accepting or changing terms.
11. Any explicit provider-displayed statement that delayed historical SIP is included, excluded, restricted, or requires another plan or classification.

The inspection may navigate only to the minimum account, profile, plans, subscription, market-data, entitlement, classification, eligibility, or existing-agreement pages necessary to establish those facts.

- linked bank information;
- payment-card or bank-account details;
- tax documents;
- identity documents;
- residential address;
- full account numbers;
- API keys or secrets;
- portfolio performance;
- watchlists;
- unrelated personal profile information.

If an authorized page incidentally displays such information, it must not be transcribed or included in the inspection evidence.

## 8. Required factual distinctions

The later inspection record must distinguish:

- directly displayed account fact;
- user interpretation;
- public-document fact;
- unresolved licensing question;
- unresolved technical question;
- information not displayed;
- information requiring a prohibited action;
- information requiring a separately authorized provider or legal clarification.

Absence of a displayed restriction must not be interpreted as permission.

A displayed entitlement must not be interpreted as authorization to call an API or retrieve market data.

## 9. Required stop conditions

The later inspection must stop without proceeding when:

1. a click may change the account, plan, subscription, entitlement, classification, or billing state;
2. an upgrade, downgrade, purchase, payment, trial, or subscription flow is encountered;
3. acceptance, acknowledgment, certification, signature, or reaffirmation of terms is required;
4. professional or nonprofessional classification must be created, changed, or recertified;
5. a form, questionnaire, support request, email, chat, or ticket submission is required;
6. API-key creation, regeneration, display, copying, or client creation is required;
7. an API call, market-data request, test request, sample request, or provider test is required;
8. SIP, IEX, or other market data would be accessed;
9. information outside the authorized scope cannot reasonably be avoided;
10. an action's read-only nature is uncertain;
11. the requested fact is not directly displayed;
12. a legal, licensing, permitted-use, storage, non-display, derived-data, publication, redistribution, reporting, or fee question remains ambiguous;
13. any unexpected material account, contractual, cost, privacy, or governance issue appears.

Encountering a stop condition does not authorize resolving it.

## 12. Unresolved licensing boundary

Account inspection may identify existing plan, classification, entitlement, subscription, and agreement facts.

It does not by itself resolve whether this project may:

- store delayed historical SIP bars;
- process the data through ML or RL systems;
- create derived data or model outputs;
- publish charts, examples, datasets, model artifacts, or performance results;
- redistribute raw or recoverable market information;
- avoid or incur non-display, reporting, licensing, or exchange fees.

These questions must be resolved or explicitly governed before any technical SIP-access authorization is considered.

Account inspection alone cannot authorize technical testing.
